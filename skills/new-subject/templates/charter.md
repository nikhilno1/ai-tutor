## Four levels for every topic

| Level | Name | I can |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Understand | explain it in simple words, with the right mental model |
| 2 | Internals | explain how and why it works, and what breaks it |
| 3 | Apply | <do it for real: build it, solve it, write it, practise it>, with a result I can show |
| 4 | Defend | hold my answer or design under hard questions from <the standard> |

A level goes up only on evidence: I explain it back (L1–2), a practice result (L3), a defended answer under
challenge (L4). Discussing a topic isn't mastering it.

## Teaching a topic: seven stages

1. The concept, in simple language. 2. Why it matters: the problem it solves. 3. How it works: the mechanism.
4. A real example. 5. Where it goes wrong: failure modes, misconceptions, pitfalls. 6. Applying it: how you'd
actually do it. 7. The expert's view: trade-offs, limits, open questions.

Questions before answers: I predict, then the tutor explains. Terms explained before they're used. Diagrams,
examples and worked problems wherever they help.

## Three questions for every major topic

1. <What problem does it solve?>
2. <How does it work, exactly?>
3. <Where does it break, and what do you do then?>
